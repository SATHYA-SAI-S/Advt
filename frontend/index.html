What This Means: Executive Business Insights

Strategic marketing investment guidance synthesized directly from dataset attribution models
Actionable Synthesis
1. Which channels contribute the most credit?
Paid Search (23.3% - 28.7%) and Direct (20.0% - 38.0%) receive the largest volume of conversion credit across all models. Paid Search acts as the critical bridge connecting mid-funnel consideration to purchase, while Direct captures repeat and high-intent brand recall.
2. Which channels assist conversions without receiving final-click credit?
Display Ads (Programmatic Retargeting) and Social Media exhibit massive assist ratios (Display Ads receives only 2.6% Last-Click credit but 13.8% Markov credit; Social Media receives 0.8% Last-Click vs 10.1% Markov credit). Cutting budgets for these channels based on Last-Click metrics would cause a severe collapse in downstream pipeline volume.
3. Which channel relationships occur most often?
The highest transition volumes occur between Display Ads → Paid Search (volume: 480 transitions) and Social Media → Paid Search / Direct (volume: 420 transitions). Programmatic ad exposure primes prospective customers to later search for brand keywords.
4. Which channel rankings change between attribution models?
Display Ads surges from #6 under Last-Click to #3 under Markov and Shapley. Conversely, Direct drops from an inflated #1 (38.0%) under Last-Click down to #2 (20.0%) under Shapley, correctly redistributing credit to the touchpoints that initiated customer interest.
5. Which estimates have the greatest uncertainty?
Display Ads and Social Media have the widest 95% bootstrap confidence halos (±2.8% to ±3.4%), reflecting variability in long multi-touch conversion paths. Conversely, Paid Search has narrow confidence intervals (±1.2%), confirming its role as a high-confidence, decision-stable investment.
6. Descriptive associations vs. supported causal conclusions?
While Last-Click and Linear allocations are purely descriptive heuristics, the Criteo Uplift benchmark confirms an 84.1% relative causal uplift for treated ad exposures. Markov removal effects model the causal counterfactual of removing each channel from customer paths, providing decision-stable ground truth for media mix optimization.
